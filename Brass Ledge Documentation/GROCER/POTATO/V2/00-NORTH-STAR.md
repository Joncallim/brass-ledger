# V2 North Star

Backlink: [[README]]

## Product finding

Human play identified the V1 headquarters loop as boring despite sound replay,
saves, staff mechanics, advice, onboarding, and pacing. More polish around
mandatory packet processing is therefore not the answer under test.

## Player fantasy and loop

The player is the commander, not a headquarters clerk. The desired thought is:

> “I caused this. I have a plan for what to do about it. I need to see what happens next.”

The loop is **Situation → Command → Consequence → Anticipation**. It must create
agency, strategic identity, uncertainty, counterplay, regret or vindication,
and a voluntary wish to continue or replay differently.

## Pillars

1. Command by exception: staff runs routine work; the commander delegates,
   intervenes, or defers a small agenda of consequential issues.
2. Standing intent: delegation follows visible commander priorities, not an
   opaque autopilot.
3. A bounded adversary pursues its own objective from hidden posture.
4. Consequences persist as concrete opportunities, exposures, promises,
   preparation, and investment—not a single moral meter.
5. At least one institutional build changes the action or information space.
6. Failure remains painful but manipulable before the final crisis.

## Anti-goals

V2 must not become a meter-optimisation puzzle, staff chatbot, generic strategy
framework, scripted drama machine, omniscient recommendation system, or
automation that chooses for the player. V2 does not prove fun through tests or
claim a human gate from synthetic play.

## What the prototype proves

The six-cycle slice tests whether command-by-exception plus standing intent,
hidden-but-fair opposition, and persistent causal history can make a player
want the next turn. It does not establish final campaign length, intervention
budget, complete doctrine, or final content breadth.
