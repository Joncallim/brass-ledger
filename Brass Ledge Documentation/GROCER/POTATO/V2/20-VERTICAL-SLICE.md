## Setting

Kestrel Strait is a six-cycle laboratory about commanding a coalition headquarters responsible for the Beacon Channel relay. Ravellan wants coalition control to become untenable before reinforcement is politically feasible, through physical seizure or political access collapse.

The commander sets four standing directions at opening: what matters most, what staff must not spend without asking, which temporary cost is tolerable, and how HQ should normally behave when several reasonable options remain.

Staff runs the headquarters by default. The prototype allows two personal interventions per cycle. A standing direction can therefore cause consequential staff action without a click; the commander's ownership comes from the direction and later consequences, not mandatory approvals.

## Six-cycle pressure arc

### Cycle 1 — Ownership

Issues:

- Beacon watch;
- partner consultation;
- Lattice protection.

Ordinary Beacon watch is detectably weak; reinforcement improves coverage but strains reserve. Formal consultation is **not** Political's universal default: it follows partner-oriented standing direction or personal intervention. It establishes a real promise **and** a useful pre-arranged consultation channel. Lattice protection begins the three-window institutional build.

The commander can often protect all three things only by accepting their real downstream costs; “three choices” is not implemented as an arbitrary rule that one must fail.

### Cycle 2 — Consequence

Ravellan's opening shipping pressure persists while the new hidden action adds bounded world effects.

Shipping choices:

- quiet escort — less disruption, weaker visible deterrence;
- visible patrol surge — reserve-heavy visible denial;
- reroute and monitor — greater civilian/political disruption in exchange for a persistent indicator-only auxiliary-tasking clue that becomes usable C3 and can outlive the generic C3 conflict.

Political choices:

- remain silent;
- joint non-attributive warning;
- unilateral public accusation.

An active C1 consultation channel plus joint warning can coordinate the visible surge and avoid its partner penalty. Public accusation always damages the partner; an active promise additionally becomes breached. Public signalling also changes what Ravellan can legitimately observe, so silence/joint/accuse are not merely tone variants.

### Cycle 3 — Doubt

HQ receives the same authored preparation indicator plus coercion indicator across hidden openings, producing `unclear + conflicted` without revealing truth.

Issues can include:

- forward reserve preparation vs hold;
- focused staging collection vs maintain coverage;
- partner reassurance where materially relevant;
- final Lattice protection.

Focused collection reads physical preparation only and can legitimately mislead when the opponent is testing/changing; it never reads hidden posture. Reassurance is a detectably coherent coalition action. Canonical live-Lattice histories expose more than two credible intervention candidates for two tokens.

### Cycle 4 — Payoff

Ravellan's visible **pressure pattern changes ambiguously** according to its actual action; it is not a scripted universal lull.

Operational Lattice unlocks one named Task Collection outside the normal intervention budget. Without Lattice, partner liaison is narrower, commander-only, costs one intervention and creates an obligation.

The main operational issue trades:

- recover reserve — improves endurance while thinning Beacon coverage;
- prepare Beacon quietly — builds denial preparation/coverage at readiness cost and is detectably targeted enough that Ravellan can suspect discovery;
- press visible advantage — spends reserve for overt denial.

C3 reassurance + C4 quiet targeted preparation forms a normal playable coherent/discovery/coverage chain that can make #99 genuine preparation back away into coercion at C5.

### Cycle 5 — Pain

Belief-qualified warning, partner authority, reserve condition and one-shot attribution collide.

The complete final-order package is atomic: issue-array order cannot decide partner authority or reserve/exposure outcome.

Partner authority resolves explicitly as `none`, `joint`, `unilateral` or `concession` from opening `pending`.

- Honour consultation preserves commitments and can produce C6 joint authority. It permits an **immediate C5** partner-sensitive action only if the C1 formal consultation channel is still active; otherwise waiting is the tempo cost.
- Act then inform buys immediate unilateral action by worsening/breaching the political relationship.
- Political concession buys immediate authority/recovery at severe terminal cost.
- If the partner already withdrew, honouring remains legal and can preserve commitment integrity without forcing a concession, but it does not restore access.

Partner-sensitive immediate actions are exactly visible Beacon reinforcement and public attribution. Quiet reinforcement does not need partner authority.

Visible vs quiet reinforcement is mechanically distinct: visible reinforcement is more politically/exhaustion-sensitive but emits an overt targeted discovery signal that, with coherent authority, can change Ravellan's C6 terminal behavior. Quiet reinforcement buys physical preparation without that deterrent signal.

Attribution is a **one-shot opportunity**. Using credible evidence in C5 produces immediate political/discovery effects but spends the opportunity; Hold And Expose is no longer available at C6. Holding preserves the final political route but gives up the immediate effect.

C5 reserve/exposure step effects aggregate then clamp once, so simultaneous reinforcement/recovery is deterministic and can intentionally trade scarce intervention attention to offset physical costs.

### Cycle 6 — Reckoning

The player sees only the overt crisis family:

- `seizure-underway`;
- `threshold-confrontation`;
- `pressure-receding`.

Prior posture/preparation history remains hidden until debrief.

Final courses test what the player built:

- **Quiet Denial** — prepared physical denial against seizure; also the restrained fallback for threshold/pressure-receding so de-escalation never forces mobilisation.
- **Joint Visible Denial** — relies on real C5 joint authority and spends final reserve.
- **Emergency Mobilisation** — brute-force comeback; can save earlier mistakes but always carries an emergency-surge severe cost when it succeeds and may further damage an uncoordinated partnership.
- **Hold And Expose** — requires an **unspent** credible attribution opportunity plus partner access; politically strong against non-seizure pressure and physically safe against seizure only if prior denial preparation/exposure supports it.

Final route effects mutate authoritative reserve/partner/attribution state **before** Pareto vector and outcome classification. A route that ends with a brittle reserve cannot hide that cost just because it began stronger.

Outcomes remain Strategic Success, Costly Success, Political Defeat and Operational Defeat. No route is universal.

## Concrete persistent state

Kestrel uses explicit records rather than a global score:

- Beacon exposure;
- Beacon preparation;
- reserve condition;
- partner consent;
- consultation promise;
- partner authority;
- political concession;
- liaison obligation;
- Lattice investment;
- attribution opportunity;
- HQ evidence/assessment;
- hidden Ravellan posture/preparation/observations under #99.

## Fairness / recovery

Before Cycle 6, serious deterioration retains authored costly counterplay where promised:

- reserve strain/brittleness → consolidation;
- partner deterioration/withdrawal → consultation/concession depending desired objective;
- Beacon weakness → preparation/reinforcement;
- missed Lattice → narrower liaison;
- poor information → focused/Lattice/liaison routes where still temporally useful.

No seed may create unavoidable pre-terminal hard loss without an earlier meaningful legal decision/countermeasure.

## Emotional arc

1. Ownership — “I chose how this headquarters should behave.”
2. Consequence — “It followed that direction and spent something.”
3. Doubt — “I still cannot tell what the opponent is doing.”
4. Payoff — “The institution/strategy I protected changed what I can do.”
5. Pain — “I cannot have tempo, political authority, readiness, information and protection all at once.”
6. Reckoning — “This crisis is testing the campaign I built.”
