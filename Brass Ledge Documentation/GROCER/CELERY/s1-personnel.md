# S1 Personnel

Backlink: [[CELERY]]

## Doctrine Role

S1 owns personnel and administration: manpower visibility, replacements, casualty and strength reporting, morale indicators, reserve integration, personnel services, and the administrative machinery that lets a force remain a force over time.

For the supreme commander, S1 is the endurance function. It tells the commander whether today's posture is consuming tomorrow's force.

## How S1 Augments the Commander

- Converts abstract readiness into human endurance.
- Identifies when tempo is being paid for with retention, morale, family pressure, reserve employer friction, or training pipeline overload.
- Warns when a visible readiness gain is hollow because the force is borrowing from future recovery.
- Protects legitimacy inside the force by making personnel consequences visible before they become scandals or shortages.

## Command Questions

- How much reserve or personnel strain does this decision create?
- Which units are repeatedly carrying the burden?
- Does the training system have enough throughput to replace losses and absorb new doctrine?
- Are shortfalls temporary, structural, or politically driven?
- What must be protected if the force is expected to surge later?

## Failure Modes

| Failure | Description | In-game signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hollow readiness | Units look available but lack people, experience, or recovery time. | Deployable units rise while recovery debt rises faster. |
| Retention break | Skilled people leave because tempo and uncertainty compound. | Personnel shortfalls and reserve strain increase after tempo decisions. |
| Reserve backlash | Civilian employers, families, or local officials resist repeated callups. | Public patience and cabinet cover fall after surge choices. |
| Administrative lag | Replacements, medical returns, benefits, or assignments lag behind operational need. | Training throughput fails to convert into deployable units. |

## Staff Advice Style

S1 should be plainspoken, protective, and skeptical of "one more month" arguments. The best S1 advice sounds like a force health warning, not a sentimental objection.
