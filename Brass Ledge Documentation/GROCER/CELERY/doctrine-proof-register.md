# Doctrine Proof Register

Backlink: [[CELERY]]

This register records the proof behind CELERY doctrine claims. Use official doctrine, government, or military education sources first. If a claim cannot be traced here or in [[doctrine-sources]], do not promote it into POTATO mechanics.

## NATO AJP-3 Staff Directorate Baseline

Source: `Allied Joint Publication-3, Allied Joint Doctrine for the Conduct of Operations`, Edition D Version 1 + UK national elements, promulgated by the NATO Standardization Office on 6 August 2025.

Proof:

- The publication says the basic organization of a joint task force headquarters is often the staff directorate.
- It lists typical staff directorates as J1 personnel and administration, J2 intelligence, J3 operations, J4 logistics, J5 plans, and J6 communications systems.
- It also notes possible additional directorates including J7 training, J8 finance, J9 civil-military cooperation, strategic communications, medical, and engineering.

Reference: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6964e72799fbdc498faecce2/AJP_3_Ed_D_V1-O.pdf

## UK PJHQ Staff Responsibilities

Source: UK Ministry of Defence, `Permanent Joint Headquarters`, GOV.UK.

Proof:

- PJHQ is described as commanding joint and multinational military operations on behalf of the Ministry of Defence.
- Its public organization lists J1 personnel, J2 operational intelligence, J3 current operations, J4 logistics/medical, J5 crisis and deliberate planning, J6 communication and information systems, J8 finance and human resources, and J9 policy, legal, and media operations.

Reference: https://www.gov.uk/government/groups/the-permanent-joint-headquarters

## UK JDP 01 NATO Context

Source: UK Ministry of Defence, `UK Joint Operations Doctrine (JDP 01)`, GOV.UK.

Proof:

- JDP 01 is described as introducing NATO operational-level doctrine and explaining the national context for a UK joint force commander and staff.
- The page notes that JDP 01 references appropriate NATO publications rather than duplicating allied doctrine.

Reference: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/campaigning-a-joint-doctrine-publication

## Netherlands No Pure Staff Structure

Source: Netherlands Ministry of Defence, `Joint Doctrine Publication 5 Command and Control`.

Proof:

- The doctrine states that no staff structures are purely function-based or process-based.
- It says there is no one-size-fits-all solution and that commanders must decide staff structure and composition before deployment.
- It emphasizes mutual recognizability for interoperability.

Reference: https://www.defensie.nl/binaries/defensie/documenten/publicaties/2012/03/16/joint-doctrine-publication-5-command-and-control-en/Joint%20Doctrine%20Publication%205%20Command%20and%20Control%20EN-1.pdf

## Netherlands Staff Functions

Source: Netherlands Ministry of Defence, `Joint Doctrine Publication 5 Command and Control`.

Proof:

- The publication identifies the traditional function-based staff model and lists personnel, intelligence, operations, logistics, plans, CIS, training, finance, and CIMIC.
- It says joint force staff elements use `J` plus the function number, while combined joint staffs use `CJ`.
- It describes staff sections as providing expertise and experience for planning, decision-making, execution, and evaluation.

Reference: https://www.defensie.nl/binaries/defensie/documenten/publicaties/2012/03/16/joint-doctrine-publication-5-command-and-control-en/Joint%20Doctrine%20Publication%205%20Command%20and%20Control%20EN-1.pdf

## Netherlands Chief Of Staff Role

Source: Netherlands Ministry of Defence, `Joint Doctrine Publication 5 Command and Control`.

Proof:

- The chief of staff is described as a key link between commander and staff.
- Main tasks include coordinating staff sections, supervising plans and orders, monitoring battle rhythm and structure, creating horizontal teams, coordinating staff-wide work, disseminating CCIR, and monitoring information management.

Reference: https://www.defensie.nl/binaries/defensie/documenten/publicaties/2012/03/16/joint-doctrine-publication-5-command-and-control-en/Joint%20Doctrine%20Publication%205%20Command%20and%20Control%20EN-1.pdf

## France CPOIA J-Branches

Source: French Ministry for the Armed Forces, `Le commandement pour les operations interarmees (CPOIA)`.

Proof:

- The public page describes CPOIA as a national operational-level joint command reference for commanding joint forces in national or multinational operations.
- It states that the planning-engagement division is modeled on a NATO-type staff and organized in J branches: J1 personnel, J2 intelligence, J3 operations, J4 logistics, J5 planning, J6 command information systems, and J8 budget and finance.

Reference: https://www.defense.gouv.fr/ema/commandement-operations-interarmees-cpoia

## China Active Defense

Source: State Council Information Office of the People's Republic of China, `China's Military Strategy`, May 2015.

Proof:

- The white paper identifies active defense as the essence of CPC military strategic thought.
- It links strategic defense with operational and tactical offense and describes a posture of not attacking unless attacked, while counterattacking if attacked.
- It emphasizes holistic national security and the CPC goal of building a strong military.

## US Army ADP 3-90 Tactical Fundamentals

Source: U.S. Army, `ADP 3-90 Offense and Defense`, July 2019. Public mirror used for repository access.

Proof:

- ADP 3-90 describes itself as doctrine for offensive and defensive operations.
- It identifies tactical fundamentals, principles of joint operations, offensive tasks, defensive tasks, forms of maneuver, defensive arrangements, transitions, control measures, and tactical enabling concepts.
- The publication is useful for CELERY because it treats tactics as a balance of offense, defense, control, transitions, and mission-specific tasks rather than a single preference for action.

Reference: https://irp.fas.org/doddir/army/adp3_90.pdf
