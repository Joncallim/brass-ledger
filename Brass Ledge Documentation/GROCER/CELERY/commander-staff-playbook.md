# Commander Staff Playbook

Backlink: [[CELERY]]

## Purpose

The staff augments the supreme commander by converting personal intent into coordinated institutional action. Staff work exists because a commander cannot simultaneously track personnel endurance, enemy uncertainty, operational execution, sustainment reality, and long-range coherence at full fidelity.

In Brass Ledger, this becomes the main decision fantasy: the player is not a spreadsheet optimizer. The player is a commander receiving imperfect staff advice, choosing where to accept risk, and living with the institutional consequences.

## Commander's Questions

| Moment | Staff question | Commander action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before guidance | What is true, uncertain, supportable, and strategically relevant? | Demand a staff estimate before choosing a course. |
| During comparison | Which staff lane becomes the limiting factor? | Compare courses by bottleneck, not only by benefit. |
| During dissent | Which chief is protecting the mission, and which is protecting their lane? | Separate real constraints from portfolio bias. |
| At commitment | What risk is being accepted explicitly? | Issue guidance with conditions and priorities. |
| After action | Which staff function absorbed the consequence? | Reward accurate warning and correct distorted incentives. |

## Playbook Rules

- A good commander does not ask every staff section to optimize everything at once.
- A staff warning is not a veto. It is a claim about where reality will collect payment.
- The commander should prefer explicit risk over hidden optimism.
- The staff should preserve options, but the commander must eventually close options and decide.
- Repeatedly overriding a staff lane should create debt, trust loss, and delayed failure.

## Practical Pattern

1. Start with S5: define the campaign logic.
2. Ask S2 what is known, estimated, and rumored.
3. Ask S3 what can be done this month.
4. Ask S4 what can be sustained next month.
5. Ask S1 what human cost becomes compounding debt.
6. Commit only after identifying the first function likely to break.

## Game Design Translation

Every major player decision should produce:

- one staff estimate,
- one S1-S5 burden forecast,
- one dissent or condition from a chief,
- one explicit accepted risk,
- one after-action consequence tied back to the staff function that warned about it.
