# Core Game And USP

## Core Fantasy

Brass Ledger is a strategic staff-command simulator about keeping a state ready, credible, legitimate, and politically survivable under worsening warning conditions. The player is not a battlefield commander moving units on a map. The player is the senior decision-maker inside a joint headquarters, shaping guidance through staff portfolios, advisers, uncertain intelligence, industrial limits, and political consequences.

## Core Aspects

| Aspect | Definition | Current engine support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strategic readiness | The force must become credible without burning out personnel or sustainment. | Strong: force-generation, sustainment, and burden penalties are implemented. |
| Institutional capacity | Staff directorates can only absorb so much work each month. | Strong: directorate burden converts choices into execution/confidence penalties. |
| Political legitimacy | Domestic cover, public patience, cabinet tolerance, and media heat constrain action. | Strong: domestic metrics and outcome checks exist. |
| Alliance credibility | Partners need reassurance and alignment, but public signaling has costs. | Medium: alliance metrics exist, but diplomacy is not yet a deep system. |
| Uncertain warning | Intelligence confidence and deception pressure shape risk. | Medium: metrics and counter-deception exist, but fog-of-war is not fully modelled. |
| Capability modernization | Programs mature through staff absorption, tags, and blockers. | Medium: capability programs exist; full dual tech tree is still planned. |
| Replayability | Turns are deterministic from state, input, and seed. | Strong: replay hashes and validation exist. |
| Character pressure | Chiefs respond according to doctrine, risk tolerance, trust, and institutional role. | Strong for text; not yet AI-planning agents. |
| Text and sprite generation | The engine generates advisor portraits and structured narrative output. | Strong for SVG sprites and authored text assembly; no bitmap pipeline yet. |

## Unique Selling Point

Brass Ledger's strongest USP is **deterrence as bureaucratic strategy**: a game where the winning move is not simply maximizing military output, but sequencing credible action through a constrained institution without losing public legitimacy, allied confidence, or escalation control.

Most strategy games make staff systems invisible. Brass Ledger makes the staff system the board. The S1-S5 functions are not flavor departments; they are the playable machinery that turns intention into executable strategy.

## Target Experience

The player should feel:

- informed but never fully certain
- powerful but institutionally constrained
- rewarded for sequencing, not spamming
- responsible for people, partners, and political trust
- tempted by visible action, then forced to account for hidden costs

## What The Game Is Not

- It is not a tactical combat game.
- It is not a pure spreadsheet optimization game.
- It is not a visual-novel adviser chat with arbitrary branching.
- It is not a grand-strategy map-painter.

It is closest to a hybrid of policy wargame, deck/tableau decision game, crisis-management sim, and relationship-driven advisory game.
