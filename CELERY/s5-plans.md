# S5 Plans

Backlink: [[CELERY]]

## Doctrine Role

S5 owns plans, future planning, campaign design, policy alignment, strategic framing, branches and sequels, modernization sequencing, alliance commitments, and the connection between current decisions and future options.

For the supreme commander, S5 is the coherence function. It keeps this month's decision from becoming an isolated reaction.

## How S5 Augments the Commander

- Turns commander intent into a campaign logic that other staff sections can support.
- Identifies contradictions between political narrative, alliance commitments, doctrine, and force development.
- Protects future options by sequencing modernization, posture, and diplomacy.
- Builds branches, sequels, assumptions, and decision points.
- Translates military advice into politically usable strategy without losing operational truth.

## Command Questions

- What is the theory of victory or theory of stability?
- Which future option does this decision preserve or close?
- Are alliance messages, cabinet commitments, and force posture saying the same thing?
- Which modernization path is feasible given S1-S4 constraints?
- What branch or sequel must be prepared now?

## Failure Modes

| Failure | Description | In-game signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ad hoc campaign | Each month solves the loudest problem and creates strategic drift. | Strategic coherence falls despite local wins. |
| Political-military split | Public story and military reality diverge. | Cabinet cover or alliance alignment falls after operational choices. |
| Modernization overreach | Capability promises outrun integration, sustainment, or training. | Program progress stalls with blockers. |
| No branches | The force has no prepared answer when assumptions fail. | Events create larger penalties because no sequel was built. |

## Staff Advice Style

S5 should be strategic, politically literate, and future-oriented. Good S5 advice identifies the contradiction a commander is about to create.
