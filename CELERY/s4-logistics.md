# S4 Logistics

Backlink: [[CELERY]]

## Doctrine Role

S4 owns logistics and sustainment: supply, maintenance, transportation, services, operational contract support, sustainment planning, stockpiles, movement control, and the support relationship between current and future operations.

For the supreme commander, S4 is the support reality function. It tests every promise against the physical system required to keep it alive.

## How S4 Augments the Commander

- Shows whether a proposed posture can be supplied, moved, repaired, fueled, and sustained.
- Converts time and distance into constraints the player can understand.
- Identifies bottlenecks before they become operational failure.
- Links military choices to industrial base and commercial market dependencies.
- Prevents S3 from treating motion as free.

## Command Questions

- What support lane is the limiting factor: stockpile, fuel, repair, lift, depot, contract, or route?
- How long can this posture be sustained before it damages future readiness?
- What is being consumed faster than it is replenished?
- Which external supply-chain constraint is likely to matter next?
- What promise should not be made publicly because it is not physically supportable?

## Failure Modes

| Failure | Description | In-game signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unsupported tempo | Operations are ordered faster than sustainment can carry them. | Lift burn rises, stockpile depth falls, depot backlog rises. |
| Depot choke | Repair and maintenance become the hidden limiter on deployable strength. | Deployable units stagnate despite spending or posture decisions. |
| Stockpile illusion | Munitions or fuel sufficiency looks adequate until a surge consumes depth. | Stockpile depth collapses after visible exercise or fires choices. |
| Market exposure | Commercial shipping or industrial inputs become strategic constraints. | External constraints worsen and reduce future options. |

## Staff Advice Style

S4 should be concrete and ledger-driven. Good S4 advice names the bottleneck, the burn rate, and the month in which the promise stops being supportable.
