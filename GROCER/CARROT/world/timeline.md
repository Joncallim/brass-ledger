# Timeline

Backlink: [[world-overview]]

## Divergence Frame

The Glasshouse Meridian is alternate historical fiction, not future history. Its divergence is institutional: earlier financial shocks, harsher infrastructure privatization, and a different pattern of regional wars left the Meridian Arc more militarized and more commercially entangled than our world.

## 1974-1988: The Port Years

The first divergence is not a battle but a shipping crisis. A fuel embargo and dockworker general strike combine with a banking panic. Governments discover that they cannot move food, fuel, or military supplies without private port operators and maritime insurers. Several states nationalize their ports; others sell them to emergency consortia backed by banks and defense firms.

Veterans later call this period the Port Years because every political movement learns the same lesson: sovereignty is partly a question of who can unload a ship.

## 1989-1998: The Broken Peace

The old continental rivalry ends without a clean winner. Instead of a broad peace dividend, several militaries are reduced unevenly while private logistics firms expand into security, data management, and emergency infrastructure. Former intelligence officers, signals engineers, shipping executives, and demobilized officers build companies that can move faster than ministries.

The states that become the [[factions/meridian-covenant]] preserve alliance habits and professional staff schools. The states that become the [[factions/veyr-directorate]] conclude that openness is a vulnerability disguised as commerce.

## 1999-2008: The Mandate Settlements

Civil wars in the inland river basin end through internationally guaranteed mandate zones. River communities receive local assemblies, demobilization payments, and monitored police forces, but borders and resource rights are drawn to keep the ports stable rather than to satisfy local legitimacy. The future [[factions/free-river-mandates]] inherit institutions designed to prevent them from becoming strong.

The settlements work for a decade because everyone is tired. They fail slowly because no one believes they are just.

## 2009-2017: The Glasshouse Buildout

Commercial cloud systems, biometric travel platforms, satellite analytics, automated ports, and integrated financial compliance systems spread across the Meridian Arc. The phrase "glasshouse" begins as a boast: investors can see cargo, credit, and risk in near real time.

Security officers turn the phrase into a warning. A glasshouse is transparent, brittle, and full of reflections. Open data improves logistics but also gives adversaries signatures to imitate, corrupt, or overload.

## 2018-2022: The Quiet Probes

Open conflict remains politically impossible, so states and companies test each other below the threshold:

- Fishing fleets appear beside survey ships.
- Port-health inspections delay strategic cargo.
- Social-media leak campaigns target reserve call-ups.
- Commercial satellite images are released out of context.
- Cyber incidents hit rail scheduling and fuel billing systems but stop short of physical destruction.
- Exercises are announced as routine while ammunition and fuel movements suggest otherwise.

This period creates the tactical culture that CELERY models: tempo is useful, but unsupported tempo creates recovery debt; deception can win time, but self-deception can lose a campaign.

## 2023: The Helix Audit

An audit of port automation and insurance data shows that the [[factions/helix-compact]] can effectively slow a mobilization without firing a shot. Meridian Covenant parliaments call for public control over critical logistics. Veyr media call the audit proof that the Covenant and Helix are one system wearing two uniforms.

The Helix Compact denies being a state. Its executives insist they are infrastructure custodians. Everyone else starts planning against them as a faction.

## 2024: The White Banner Winter

Food, fuel, and fertilizer prices spike after a sequence of storms and refinery accidents. The [[factions/sanctum-league]] organizes escorted humanitarian convoys under white banners through contested waters. Veyr vessels shadow the convoys; Covenant aircraft escort them at distance; Helix insurers raise premiums; river militias steal cargo inland.

The convoys prevent famine but normalize military escort of commercial shipping.

## 2025: The Kestrel Rehearsals

Exercises around the Northern Frontier become more elaborate. A Covenant-led rehearsal tests rapid reinforcement of partner islands. Veyr theater commands test anti-access networks and civil mobilization. Sanctum coastal defense units practice cable repair escort. Helix contractors deploy autonomous port-security drones. Free River commanders learn how to disrupt rail nodes without holding them.

None of the exercises are illegal. All of them look like preparation for something worse.
