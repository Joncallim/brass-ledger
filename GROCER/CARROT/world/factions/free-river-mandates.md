# Free River Mandates

Backlink: [[../faction-balance]]

## Capsule

The Free River Mandates are a loose confederation of inland assemblies, veterans' leagues, transport unions, militia commanders, and black-market brokers descended from the Mandate Settlements. They are not a single army. They are a grievance network that can become a theater problem overnight.

## Historical Backstory

The Mandate Settlements ended the river wars by freezing them. International monitors drew administrative zones around rail bridges, fertilizer depots, locks, grain terminals, and ethnic enclaves. Local leaders received flags, stipends, police titles, and disarmament ceremonies, but the resource map remained designed around port stability.

The first postwar generation learned to survive inside artificial weakness. They built smuggling routes around customs posts, used veterans' charities as mobilization lists, turned trucking unions into intelligence networks, and learned which bridge outage could make a ministry answer the phone.

The Free River idea began as a legal campaign for toll revenue and water rights. It radicalized after repeated droughts, corrupt reconstruction audits, and the White Banner Winter, when river communities watched international convoys protect island fuel faster than inland food shipments.

## Society And Politics

The Mandates are politically fragmented but emotionally coherent. Their shared story is that outsiders call them unstable while profiting from the order that keeps them poor.

Local commanders have legitimacy where they deliver fuel, grain, security, and revenge. Central councils exist, but command authority is negotiated rather than assumed.

## Doctrine Culture

The Mandates favor:

- Economy of force.
- Sabotage and raids against logistics.
- Local intelligence.
- Political bargaining through disruption.
- Decentralized cells.
- Narrative exploitation of opponent overreaction.

They avoid conventional fights unless terrain, population, and timing make the opponent's strength irrelevant.

## S1-S5 Profile

| Function | Faction expression |
| --- | --- |
| S1 | Strong local recruitment through grievance and kinship. Poor personnel administration and high factional discipline problems. |
| S2 | Excellent local human intelligence. Weak technical collection and vulnerable to planted rumors. |
| S3 | Agile raids and disruption. Poor sustained synchronization across regions. |
| S4 | Can live off local networks for short actions. Cannot support prolonged mass without external patrons. |
| S5 | Powerful justice narrative. Weak strategic coherence when local bargains conflict. |

## Gameplay Strengths

- Generates sabotage, unrest, and rail/bridge disruption events.
- High economy-of-force impact.
- Hard to deter through conventional posture alone.
- Punishes overreaction and public-commitment errors.

## Gameplay Weaknesses

- Low interoperability and weak central command.
- Poor sustained operations.
- Vulnerable to local concessions paired with security and repair.
- Factional splits can collapse a campaign plan.

## Signature Dilemmas

- Keep disruption decentralized, or build a central command that enemies can target.
- Accept Veyr or Helix support, or preserve independence.
- Attack symbolic targets for narrative value, or boring logistics nodes for campaign effect.

## Internal Factions

- Toll Reformers want legal revenue rights and autonomy.
- Lock Brigades control river infrastructure and favor coercive bargaining.
- Veterans' Leagues want recognition, pensions, and revenge for settlement-era betrayals.
- Market Families want routes kept profitable and dislike ideological escalation.

## Narrative Voice

Mandate documents should sound local, bitter, practical, and historically specific. They should not sound like generic rebels. Their politics are about water, tolls, bridges, grain, debt, and memory.
