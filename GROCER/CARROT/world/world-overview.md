# World Overview

Backlink: [[../CARROT]]

## Working Title

The setting is the Glasshouse Meridian, a near-present alternate Earth built for Brass Ledger. Modern civilization has broadly reached today's level of technology: satellites, drones, precision fires, cyber operations, social media, electric grids, commercial cloud infrastructure, global shipping, semiconductor supply chains, high-resolution surveillance, and professional armed forces. No time travel, alien intervention, magic, or far-future super-science exists.

The speculative layer is political and institutional rather than technological. The world diverged after a sequence of late-20th-century crises produced different alliances, more privatized infrastructure, and harder regional security blocs. Most weapons, sensors, and logistics systems are recognizable to a modern observer, but the command problem is harsher because every state depends on civilian networks it does not fully control.

## Genre Pillars

The world borrows design lessons from successful Earth-based military strategy games and grounded science fiction without copying their settings:

- A small number of strongly asymmetric factions should be easier to understand than a flat map of dozens of similar states.
- Logistics, morale, political legitimacy, industry, and intelligence should matter as much as battlefield strength.
- War should begin through a plausible trigger: a contested incident, bad assumptions, domestic pressure, and alliance commitments converging faster than leaders can unwind them.
- Factions should have sincere internal histories. The player should understand why each faction thinks its behavior is defensive.
- Technology should increase ambiguity instead of solving it. Sensors create evidence, but deception, interpretation, data access, and political incentives decide what commanders believe.

## Strategic Situation

The Glasshouse Meridian is centered on the Meridian Arc, a belt of ports, island chains, cold-water straits, undersea data cables, rare-earth refining sites, agricultural chokepoints, and high-capacity energy interconnectors. The Arc is not the richest place on Earth, but it is where several systems touch:

- Northern shipping lanes that carry fuel, grain, and industrial inputs.
- Eastern fabrication corridors that produce trusted electronics, optics, and guidance components.
- Inland river basins that move bulk food and fertilizer.
- Maritime insurance hubs that decide whether cargo moves during crisis.
- Partner states with capable militaries but fragile domestic consent.

Before the war, the international order is not peaceful. It is a managed competition held together by trade, debt, public fatigue, and the memory of previous proxy wars. The major powers have learned to probe below the threshold of open conflict: coast guard harassment, militia roadblocks, port slowdowns, leak campaigns, selective cyber disruption, exercises near disputed waters, and industrial denials framed as safety inspections.

## The Core Conflict

The war begins after the Kestrel Run Incident, a chain of events around a commercial convoy, an undersea cable repair operation, and a disputed maritime exclusion zone. The initial deaths are few. The consequences are enormous because each faction sees the incident through a different historical trauma:

- The [[factions/meridian-covenant]] sees a test of alliance credibility.
- The [[factions/veyr-directorate]] sees encirclement by commercial-military systems.
- The [[factions/helix-compact]] sees a threat to the private infrastructure that keeps states alive.
- The [[factions/sanctum-league]] sees another great-power crisis exported into its home waters.
- The [[factions/free-river-mandates]] sees a chance to renegotiate the postwar river order.
- [[factions/gray-signal-international]] sees a market for deniable leverage.

## Tone

The setting should read like a military-political wiki assembled from staff papers, declassified histories, academic monographs, and faction internal narratives. It should avoid heroic destiny. Leaders are constrained by reserves, budgets, pride, fear, and institutional memory. Most actors believe they are preventing a worse war.

## Gameplay Fit

The world exists to feed Brass Ledger's S1-S5 design:

| Staff function | Worldbuilding expression |
| --- | --- |
| S1 Personnel | Professional forces are small, reserves are politically sensitive, and long crises hollow institutions before open battle does. |
| S2 Intelligence | Almost every faction manipulates open-source signals, industrial indicators, and exercise patterns. Confidence is contested. |
| S3 Operations | Visible posture can deter, reassure, provoke, or expose hollow readiness depending on support and warning. |
| S4 Logistics | Shipping, fuel, propellant, depot throughput, electronics, and commercial contracts are strategic terrain. |
| S5 Plans | Coherent sequencing matters because public narratives, alliances, and modernization promises can contradict each other. |
