# Tactical Doctrine Patterns

Backlink: [[CELERY]]

## Purpose

This note captures tactical doctrine as design patterns for Brass Ledger. It does not tell the player how to fight a real battle. It identifies recurring military ideas that can become strategic staff arguments, faction differences, and POTATO mechanics.

Evidence for these patterns is recorded in [[doctrine-proof-register]].

## Core Tactical Tensions

| Pattern | Doctrine idea | Staff argument | Failure if overused |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Objective | Action should serve a clear, attainable purpose. | S5 asks whether this month has a coherent aim. | Activity without campaign logic. |
| Initiative | Forces seek to seize, retain, and exploit initiative. | S3 wants tempo before the adversary sets terms. | Tempo outruns S1/S4 and becomes fragility. |
| Mass and concentration | Combat power matters most at decisive places and times. | S3/S5 argue for a main effort. | Secondary lanes starve; deception becomes easier. |
| Economy of force | Secondary efforts get only enough resources to serve the main effort. | S5 forces the commander to name what will not be optimized. | Hidden risk collects in neglected lanes. |
| Maneuver | Advantage comes from placing the opponent in a disadvantageous position. | S3 seeks options that change the adversary's problem. | Movement becomes theater if not supported. |
| Surprise and deception | Advantage may come from time, place, method, ambiguity, or concealment. | S2/S5 argue over whether the plan depends on secrecy or misdirection. | False confidence, escalation, or self-deception. |
| Security | The force should deny the adversary unexpected advantage. | S2 warns about exposure, assumptions, and counter-moves. | Over-security slows initiative and narrows options. |
| Simplicity | Plans and orders should be clear enough to execute under stress. | S3 resists clever plans that cannot be synchronized. | Elegant plans fail at handoff points. |
| Tempo | Relative speed of decision and action can create advantage. | S3 pushes pace; S1/S4 price the burn. | Culmination arrives early. |
| Culmination | A force reaches a point where it cannot continue effectively. | S4 and S1 identify the ceiling before the map does. | The player discovers the limit after committing. |
| Reserve | Retained capacity lets the commander exploit or recover. | S1/S3 argue for slack rather than full commitment. | Full commitment leaves no answer to events. |
| Combined arms/interdependence | Different capabilities protect and enable each other. | S3 coordinates, S4 supports, S2 validates, S5 sequences. | Single-lane optimization becomes brittle. |

## S1-S5 Tactical Debate

The same tactical idea should sound different through each staff function:

| Tactical idea | S1 Personnel | S2 Intelligence | S3 Operations | S4 Logistics | S5 Plans |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tempo | Can people sustain this pace? | Are we acting faster than we understand? | Can we set the rhythm? | Can fuel, lift, repair, and stocks keep up? | Does speed serve the campaign? |
| Deception | Does secrecy strain trust or readiness? | What is known, false, or planted? | Can the action remain synchronized under ambiguity? | What support signature gives the plan away? | What story survives exposure? |
| Main effort | Which units and people carry the burden? | Which uncertainty affects the decisive point? | Where do we concentrate effort? | Which support lane gets priority? | What future option are we buying? |
| Reserve | Which people must not be spent? | What unknown requires a hedge? | What force remains uncommitted? | What stock/lift capacity remains? | What political or alliance option remains? |
| Simplicity | Can the personnel system administer it? | Can assumptions be explained cleanly? | Can the force execute the order? | Can support elements understand priority? | Can cabinet and allies repeat the logic? |

## Adversarial Debate Example

## Thesis

Generator: High tempo should always reward the player because initiative is central to tactical success.

## Attack

Discriminator: The claim overreaches. MCDP 1 supports tempo and maneuver, but ADP 3-90 and NATO land doctrine also make clear that tactics include offense and defense, control, security, sustainment, and context. Tempo without support, people, and intelligence becomes self-harm.

## Surviving Rule

Refiner: Tempo is powerful only when it creates relative advantage faster than it creates recovery debt, support burn, uncertainty, and political exposure.

## POTATO Translation

Integrator: Model tempo as a multiplier gated by S1 recovery debt, S2 confidence, S3 executable posture, S4 supportable tempo, and S5 coherence. High tempo should be attractive, but repeated high tempo should produce culmination.

## Design Rules

- Tactical concepts become staff debates before they become math.
- Every tactical bonus needs an exhaustion, uncertainty, support, or coherence counterweight.
- The game should reward sequencing more than maximal action.
- Tactics should create "what breaks first?" questions, not isolated modifiers.
