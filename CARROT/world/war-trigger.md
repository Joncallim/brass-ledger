# War Trigger: The Kestrel Run Incident

Backlink: [[world-overview]]

## Summary

The Kestrel Run Incident is the trigger point for the main Brass Ledger war. It is not a planned invasion. It is an escalation spiral caused by a convoy, a cable-repair mission, competing exclusion zones, bad intelligence, and leaders who fear that backing down will destroy their faction's credibility.

## Geography

The Kestrel Run is a narrow sea lane in the [[theaters/northern-frontier]]. It contains:

- Two high-capacity undersea data cables.
- A shallow-water gas connector.
- A disputed inspection zone claimed by Veyr-aligned maritime authorities.
- A Sanctum League island community dependent on imported fuel.
- A Helix-operated automated transshipment terminal.
- Covenant partner patrol routes.

No single asset is worth a general war. Together they are a condensed version of the Glasshouse Meridian.

## Immediate Pre-Crisis

Three weeks before the incident, seismic sensors report a fault near Cable Pair K-17. Helix engineers request a repair window. The Sanctum League asks for a fuel convoy to move during the same weather gap. The Meridian Covenant agrees to provide distant air and maritime overwatch. Veyr authorities announce a safety exclusion zone after claiming that unmanned underwater vehicles were detected near the gas connector.

S2 estimates diverge:

- Covenant analysts assess the Veyr exclusion zone as coercive but probably calibrated.
- Veyr theater staff assess the convoy as a cover for sensor emplacement.
- Helix risk officers assess the military risk correctly but underestimate political commitment.
- Sanctum commanders assess that delay will cause a domestic energy emergency.
- Free River observers see the convoy as proof that outsiders will protect cables faster than river food shipments.

## The Incident

On the second night of the repair window:

1. A Helix repair vessel loses authenticated navigation updates after a cyber intrusion into its shore relay.
2. A Veyr maritime drone broadcasts a warning using an outdated boundary file.
3. A Sanctum escort boat moves to shield the repair vessel and illuminates the drone.
4. A Covenant patrol aircraft detects a missile-control radar emission but cannot confirm whether it is live tracking or spoofed.
5. A Veyr coastal battery goes to a higher alert state after receiving manipulated open-source footage that appears to show armed divers near the gas connector.
6. A collision occurs between the repair vessel and an unmanned surface craft.
7. The cable repair boom drops, cutting part of Cable Pair K-17 and disabling two financial authentication routes.

The first casualties are engineers and sailors, not soldiers in a planned offensive. Within hours, every faction has a different truth.

## Why It Escalates

The Kestrel Run Incident escalates because the staff logic of each faction points in a different direction:

| Faction | Believed lesson | Escalatory pressure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [[factions/meridian-covenant]] | Alliance overwatch failed because it was too restrained. | Increase visible posture and reassure partners. |
| [[factions/veyr-directorate]] | The convoy was an intelligence operation hidden inside repair work. | Seize initiative before encirclement hardens. |
| [[factions/helix-compact]] | States are about to militarize private infrastructure without compensation or control. | Use contracts, insurance, and data access as leverage. |
| [[factions/sanctum-league]] | Great powers turned home waters into a test range. | Assert local control and mobilize territorial defense. |
| [[factions/free-river-mandates]] | The outside order protects cables and ports before people. | Expand sabotage and bargaining pressure inland. |
| [[factions/gray-signal-international]] | Confusion has market value. | Sell access, plausible deniability, and false certainty. |

## Campaign Start State

The player's opening campaign should begin after the incident but before full mobilization. The staff problem is not "win the war this turn." It is:

- Recover credible warning.
- Keep partners aligned.
- Avoid a visible posture that outruns executable readiness.
- Protect reserves from political backlash.
- Identify which logistics constraint will become decisive.
- Preserve a campaign story that survives leaks and contradictory evidence.
